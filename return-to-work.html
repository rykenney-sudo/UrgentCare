Employer Services · Return to Work Evaluations

Return-to-work evaluationsfor a safe comeback.

When an employee returns after an extended absence—an illness, an injury, surgery, or a mental-health leave—we confirm they can safely take their job back on. We measure their recovery against the role's real demands and give you a clear, documented answer.

What the Evaluation Delivers

  • A clear fit-for-duty determination
  • Capabilities measured against the job
  • Graduated-return or accommodation plans

Evaluations are arranged with employers and typically paid by the company. Call us to set one up for an employee.

Why a return-to-work evaluation matters

Bringing someone back too soon risks re-injury, a longer recovery, and more lost time; holding them back without reason costs everyone. A return-to-work evaluation settles it on evidence—comparing where the employee is in their recovery to what the job truly demands—so you get a clear, defensible answer and they get back to work safely.

See how it works ↓

When to request one

  • After an extended medical leave
  • After a work injury, before resuming full duty
  • After surgery or a hospitalization
  • After a mental-health leave
  • For a safety-sensitive role
  • When restrictions or accommodations may be needed

Have the employee bring any restrictions or notes from their treating provider—it makes for a faster, more accurate evaluation.

How it works

A clear answer: can this employee safely return?

The provider assesses the employee's recovery, compares it to the essential functions of their role, and issues a specific recommendation—clearance, a graduated plan, or accommodations—documented for your records.

Capabilities assessed

Strength, movement, endurance, and—where relevant—cognitive recovery are measured against the job.

Matched to the job

Recovery is compared directly to the essential, day-to-day tasks the role actually requires.

Clear recommendation

You receive a specific outcome—full clearance, a graduated return, or defined accommodations.

What the evaluation assesses

The provider measures the employee's recovery against what the job actually requires:

  • Physical capabilities—strength, movement, and endurance
  • Mental and cognitive recovery, where relevant
  • Overall recovery progress since the absence
  • The essential, day-to-day demands of the specific role
  • Whether any restrictions or supports are needed

Possible outcomes

  • Full clearance—ready to return to regular duties
  • Graduated return—a phased ramp-up of hours or workload
  • Accommodations—specific changes to tasks, schedule, or setup
  • Not yet ready—with a clear plan to re-evaluate

For employers

  • Clear, written fit-for-duty documentation for your file
  • Any work restrictions or accommodations spelled out
  • Coordination with the employee's treating providers
  • Helps prevent re-injury, lost time, and repeat absences

Good to know

The goal is a safe, durable return—protecting the employee from re-injury and keeping the workplace running. It isn't a hurdle; it's a checkpoint that benefits everyone.

Bring the job description or essential functions and any notes from the employee's treating provider. The more we know about the role and recovery, the more accurate the evaluation.
