Prompt care, accurate claims

Our goal is to provide prompt, high-quality medical care while ensuring claims are submitted accurately and in accordance with applicable insurance requirements and state regulations. Because MVA claims are handled differently than routine visits, we collect your accident and coverage details up front—which helps minimize billing delays and claim-processing issues later.

Patient responsibilities

Please provide the following at the time of registration:

  • Automobile insurance company name
  • Motor vehicle accident (MVA) claim number
  • Claim adjuster's name (if assigned)
  • Claim adjuster's telephone number (if available)
  • Date of the accident
  • Attorney information (if represented)
  • Government-issued photo identification

Providing complete and accurate information at the time of your visit helps minimize billing delays and reduces the likelihood of claim-processing issues.

How it works

How a motor vehicle accident visit works

From registration through claim submission, here's what to expect when you come in for care after an accident.

Bring your claim details

At registration, share your auto insurance, MVA claim number, adjuster details, accident date, and a photo ID so we can bill the correct carrier.

$210 deposit at registration

A $210 deposit is collected before you're seen for any MVA-related visit. It's refunded if your carrier pays the claim in full.

We submit the claim for you

After your visit, we file the claim with the auto insurance carrier tied to your accident, using the information you provide.

How the $210 deposit works

  • A $210 deposit is required prior to being seen for all MVA-related visits.
  • After your visit, we submit the claim to the auto insurance carrier associated with your accident.
  • If the carrier pays the claim in full, your deposit is refunded to the original payment method whenever possible.
  • If the carrier partially pays or denies the claim, your deposit is applied to your balance and you remain responsible for any unpaid amount.

Patient financial responsibility

Submission of a claim to an auto insurance carrier does not guarantee payment. Patients are ultimately responsible for all charges not paid by the responsible carrier—including claims that are denied, delayed, limited by policy coverage, or otherwise determined to be the patient's responsibility.

If the carrier requests additional information after your visit, timely cooperation may be necessary to avoid delays in claim processing.

Questions?

If you have questions about your motor vehicle accident claim or the billing process, our registration staff will be happy to assist you—before or after your visit.
