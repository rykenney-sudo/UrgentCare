Why you might need an X-Ray

An X-Ray is a quick, painless scan that lets our clinicians see bones and parts of the chest and abdomen that a physical exam can't reveal. It's one of the fastest ways to tell a break from a bad sprain, check a nagging cough, or find the source of pain that isn't going away—so you leave with answers and a clear plan, not a guess.

Common reasons to get one

  • A fall, twist, or blow that may have broken a bone
  • Swelling, bruising, or pain you can't put weight on
  • A sprain or sports injury that isn't improving
  • A persistent cough or chest congestion (possible pneumonia)
  • Something swallowed, or an object lodged in soft tissue
  • Unexplained pain that hasn't settled after a few days

Having a medical emergency—like trouble breathing, a deformed limb, or heavy bleeding? Call 911 or go to the nearest ER.

How insurers bill radiology

NOTE: Some insurance carrier policies require radiology services to be billed in two components: the technical component (e.g., the performance of the X-ray) and the professional component (radiologist interpretation). Although these components may be billed separately, the total reimbursement is generally consistent with the amount listed in the fee schedule below. Please note that carrier policies are subject to change, and in some instances, reimbursement may slightly exceed the listed fee.
