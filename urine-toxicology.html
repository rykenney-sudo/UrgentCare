Why a urine tox screen

Urine toxicology is the most common way to screen for recent drug use—quick, non-invasive, and able to cover a broad panel from a single sample. We give you a rapid on-site result for fast decisions, with the option of chain-of-custody collection and lab confirmation for anything that needs to stand up to scrutiny.

When it's used

  • Pre-employment screening, especially safety-critical roles
  • Post-accident or post-incident
  • Random testing in a workplace program
  • Reasonable suspicion
  • Return-to-duty and follow-up
  • Pain-management or treatment monitoring

A rapid screen is a presumptive result—a positive should be confirmed by the lab before it's treated as final.

5-Panel Drug Screen Options

For drug testing, we offer two options:

In House Drug Screen

  • Testing is performed on-site with results available during the visit.
  • The 5-panel screen tests for commonly screened substances, including marijuana (THC), cocaine, opiates, amphetamines, and PCP.
  • This result simply yields a positive/negative result for each component.

Laboratory Drug Screen

  • The specimen is collected at our office and sent to Quest Diagnostics, a SAMHSA-certified laboratory, for testing and confirmation when applicable.

What a standard panel detects

Panels vary by program. A typical workplace screen looks for:

  • Amphetamines and methamphetamines
  • Barbiturates and benzodiazepines
  • Cocaine
  • Marijuana (THC / cannabinoids)
  • Opioids—heroin, morphine, fentanyl, oxycodone
  • Phencyclidine (PCP)
  • Alcohol (ethanol or metabolites such as EtG)

How it works

  • You provide a urine sample in a clinical collection cup
  • A rapid immunoassay screen flags broad classes of drugs in minutes
  • Any positive can be confirmed with GC-MS to identify the exact compound
  • Most substances are detectable for several days—longer with heavy use
  • Chain-of-custody and MRO review are available when results must be defensible

Good to know

A rapid screen gives a presumptive result. A positive should be confirmed by the lab—and reviewed by a Medical Review Officer against any valid prescription—before it's treated as final.

Beyond employment, urine toxicology is also used clinically—for overdose evaluation and for pain-management or treatment monitoring. We test to your program's requirements; expanded panels and confirmation go to our reference lab.
