Why employers send injured
workers to us

For most on-the-job injuries, an ER means hours in a waiting room and a hospital-sized bill. We get your employee seen right away, x-rayed and treated on-site, and back to you with a clear work-status report—often the same hour. We handle the workers'-comp billing and keep you and your adjuster updated, so your team can focus on recovery and getting back to work.

Common workplace injuries we treat

  • Cuts, lacerations, and puncture wounds
  • Sprains, strains, and pulled muscles
  • Fractures and dislocations
  • Burns, bruises, and contusions
  • Eye injuries and foreign-body removal
  • Back and repetitive-strain injuries

Severe bleeding, a head injury, chest pain, or trouble breathing is an emergency—call 911, don't drive to urgent care.

How a visit works

Care that gets your team back to work

Most workplace injuries are handled start to finish in a single walk-in visit—examined, x-rayed or tested on-site, treated, and documented—with the billing and reporting handled for you.

On-site X-rays & labs

Digital X-ray and rapid lab testing in-house, so most injuries are diagnosed and treated in one visit—no second trip.

Workers' comp billed directly

We bill your workers'-compensation carrier and handle the paperwork—not your injured employee.

Same-day work-status reports

Clear return-to-work or restricted-duty documentation for you and your employee before they leave.

Injured at work? What to do

  • Report the injury to your supervisor right away
  • Come straight in—or call 911 if it's severe
  • Bring your employer and claim or insurance details
  • We examine, treat, and document the injury in one visit

For employers

  • We bill and coordinate with your workers'-comp carrier
  • Post-incident drug and urine drug testing
  • Same-day work-status and restricted-duty reports
  • Help getting employees safely back to work

Good to know

We keep your employer and claims adjuster in the loop on each visit—diagnosis, treatment, and any work restrictions—so there are no surprises and no chasing paperwork.

For anything life- or limb-threatening—severe bleeding, a head injury, chest pain, or trouble breathing—call 911 first. Urgent care is for everything short of a true emergency.
